(sec:atacama)=
# Explore the Atacama Soil Microbiome

(sec:datasets)=

Begin by looking at the samples and their metadata. You will use elevation as a
regression outcome, vegetation presence as a classification outcome, and several
measured covariates when estimating microbial association networks.

The [15-ASV walkthrough](s2-01.md) starts from a prepared feature table and
provides the inputs and commands in the order you need them. If you also want to
process the sequencing reads, follow the upstream Atacama workflow linked below.
You do not need to rerun read processing to follow the examples here.

We use the 16S rRNA Atacama soil microbiome data from {cite:t}`neilson2017significant`, deposited at the European Nucleotide Archive under project accession PRJEB17617 (secondary study accession ERP019482). The samples were collected along the Baquedano and Yungay transects in northern Chile, where elevation, soil humidity, temperature, and vegetation vary across the aridity gradient. The public QIIME 2 Atacama soils tutorial (<https://docs.qiime2.org/2024.10/tutorials/atacama-soils/>) provides the upstream amplicon-processing workflow for these data: multiplexed reads are imported, demultiplexed, subsampled, filtered by per-sample read count, denoised with DADA2 {cite:p}`callahan2016dada2`, and summarized as an ASV feature table in QIIME 2 {cite:p}`bolyen2019reproducible`. The tutorial also assigns taxonomy to the representative sequences using SILVA {cite:p}`quast2012silva` and **q2-feature-classifier** {cite:p}`bokulich2018q2`.

The analysis starts from the processed ASV feature table and the matching sample metadata produced by the upstream QIIME 2 workflow. The original sample metadata describe 75 registered samples. In the upstream QIIME 2 tutorial, samples with fewer than 100 forward reads after subsampling are removed before DADA2 denoising. The processed feature table used here therefore contains 54 samples and 1109 ASVs. All 54 samples in this table have matching metadata; the remaining metadata rows are not present in the ASV table and are outside the analysis population considered below.

## Inspect the Covariates

Look for covariates that change together in the correlation matrix. The outlined
heatmap cells mark missing values replaced by the covariate mean. Keep these
imputed entries in mind when interpreting relationships with the ASVs.

Figure {ref}`S1 <fig:atacama-covariates-overview>` summarizes the numeric covariates available for the 54 analysis samples. The covariate matrix is displayed after mean imputation, using within-covariate z-scores for color and reporting the corresponding original-scale minima, medians, and maxima. The same transformed covariates are then summarized by their clustered Pearson correlation matrix. For the continuous outcome screen, missing metadata values are mean-imputed before fitting: `toc` and `ec` each have 11 imputed values, the humidity and temperature summaries each have three imputed values, and the remaining numeric outcomes used here are complete in the 54-sample table.
