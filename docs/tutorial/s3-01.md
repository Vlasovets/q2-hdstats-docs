# Postnatal infant age prediction with **q2-classo**

We predict postnatal age from the mOTU composition. Four infants (J10, J12, J17, and J22; eight samples) are held out before transformation and model selection; the remaining 26 samples from 14 infants form the training set. The split is subject-disjoint, so a child contributes samples to only one set. The feature tables retain the same 60 mOTU coordinates, including columns that are empty in the test set. Counts are transformed separately with CLR after zero replacement by 0.5. Cross-validation and stability selection use only training data.

As in the Atacama regression examples, we show model selection first and then the corresponding refit and predictions. We compare ordinary sparse log-contrast (`slc`) regression with `trac`. `slc` uses the 60 terminal mOTUs as log-contrast components. `trac` uses `add-taxa` to add taxonomy-derived aggregates and their weights to the same CLR design. Both fits use R3 concomitant regression, five-fold cross-validation on a 60-point logarithmic $\lambda/\lambda_{\max}$ grid from 1 to 0.05 (seed 1), the minimum-CV-MSE rule, and stability selection based on 100 half-samples with $q=10$ and threshold 0.4. The held-out samples illustrate one reproducible subject-disjoint split.

**Example:**

```bash
# Transform the training and held-out tables separately.
qiime classo transform-features \
    --i-features motus-train.qza --p-transformation clr --p-coef 0.5 \
    --o-x motus-clr-train.qza

qiime classo transform-features \
    --i-features motus-test.qza --p-transformation clr --p-coef 0.5 \
    --o-x motus-clr-test.qza

# SLC model selection and prediction
qiime classo regress \
    --i-features motus-clr-train.qza \
    --m-y-file outcomes.tsv --m-y-column host_age_days \
    --p-concomitant --p-path --p-path-nlam-log 60 --p-path-lamin-log 0.05 \
    --p-cv --p-cv-subsets 5 --p-cv-seed 1 --p-no-cv-one-se \
    --p-cv--nlam 60 --p-cv-lamin 0.05 --p-cv-logscale \
    --p-stabsel --p-stabsel-seed 1 --p-stabsel-b 100 --p-stabsel-q 10 \
    --p-stabsel-threshold 0.4 --p-no-lamfixed \
    --o-result motus-age-slc-r3.qza

qiime classo predict \
    --i-features motus-clr-test.qza --i-problem motus-age-slc-r3.qza \
    --o-predictions motus-age-slc-r3-test-predictions.qza
```

The `slc` minimum-CV-MSE refit retains three terminal mOTUs: *Escherichia* and *Bifidobacterium* with positive coefficients, and *Staphylococcus* with a negative coefficient. These terms form a zero-sum log-contrast (Figures {ref}`S17 <fig:motus-slc-selection>` and {ref}`S18 <fig:motus-slc-refit>`). It has $r=0.52$ and RMSE $=11.3$ days on training samples, and $r=0.72$ and RMSE $=9.3$ days for the held-out infants.

For `trac`, the taxonomy-derived design and weights are constructed before applying the same training-only selection workflow.

**Example:**

```bash
qiime classo add-taxa \
    --i-features motus-clr-train.qza --i-taxa motus-taxonomy.qza \
    --o-x motus-clr-trac-train.qza --o-aweights motus-trac-weights.qza

qiime classo regress \
    --i-features motus-clr-trac-train.qza --i-weights motus-trac-weights.qza \
    --m-y-file outcomes.tsv --m-y-column host_age_days \
    --p-concomitant --p-path --p-path-nlam-log 60 --p-path-lamin-log 0.05 \
    --p-cv --p-cv-subsets 5 --p-cv-seed 1 --p-no-cv-one-se \
    --p-cv--nlam 60 --p-cv-lamin 0.05 --p-cv-logscale \
    --p-stabsel --p-stabsel-seed 1 --p-stabsel-b 100 --p-stabsel-q 10 \
    --p-stabsel-threshold 0.4 --p-no-lamfixed \
    --o-result motus-age-trac-r3.qza
```

The `trac` minimum-CV-MSE refit selects a positive *Enterobacterales* term and a negative *Bacteroidetes* term, which form a direct taxonomic balance. It has $r=0.47$ and RMSE $=11.7$ days on training samples and $r=0.47$ and RMSE $=11.7$ days for held-out infants (Figures {ref}`S19 <fig:motus-trac-selection>` and {ref}`S20 <fig:motus-trac-refit>`). In this small split, `slc` has lower held-out error, whereas `trac` gives a more immediately taxonomically interpretable contrast. The direction of this balance is consistent with early-life microbial succession: enterobacteria are prominent among initial colonizers, whereas Bacteroidetes expand with dietary diversification and maturation of the infant gut community {cite:p}`arrieta2014intestinal`. This contextualizes the age-prediction contrast; it does not establish a causal role for either taxon.

The stability-selected refit retains four taxonomy-derived terms: positive coefficients for *Enterobacterales* and Actinobacteria, and negative coefficients for Bacteroidetes and Firmicutes (Figure {ref}`S21 <fig:motus-trac-stabsel-refit>`). The resulting predictions have $r=0.50$ and RMSE $=11.5$ days in the training data, and $r=0.53$ and RMSE $=11.3$ days in the subject-disjoint test set. The eight-sample test set yields an imprecise estimate of generalization performance.
