# Metagenomics example: the MAP study

We next illustrate how the workflow can be applied to whole-genome shotgun metagenomic data, where mOTUs are estimated from universal single-copy marker genes. The samples originate from the MAP study, *The impact of maternal asthma on the preterm infants' gut metabolome and microbiome* {cite:p}`baitong2022map`. They are available as Qiita study 13241 {cite:p}`gonzalez2018qiita`, deposited at the European Nucleotide Archive under accession PRJEB52147. We use the fecal samples and mOTUs3 profiles {cite:p}`ruscheweyh2022motus`. The analysis uses the complete merged mOTU table, with each filtering decision recorded in QIIME 2.

The raw profile has 36 samples and 379 columns. Its `unassigned` column is the complement of the fraction profiled by mOTUs rather than a taxon; it accounts for 1.8% of the marker-gene inserts and is removed explicitly. We retain samples with at least 100 assigned inserts, then mOTUs present in at least four of the remaining 34 samples (10%). No top-abundance filter or second prevalence filter is applied. The resulting $34\times60$ table remains high-dimensional ($p=60>n=34$), while excluding nearly absent features (Table {ref}`S7 <tab:motus-filtering>`).
